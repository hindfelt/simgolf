# Original facilities — manual recheck

Read the supplied English manual, PDF spreads 11–12 (printed pages 18–21), on 5 September 2026. Source: `resources/SidMeiersSimGolf-Manual (1).pdf`.

- Tennis Court (p.19): owning the facility ensures golfers start at no less than a yellow attitude rating. This is a starting-condition effect, not a documented in-round tennis-service visit. Links substitutes Stable; Desert Spa; Tropical Swim Club. The numerical mapping to the prototype mood scale is not provided.
- Clubhouse/path section (p.18): most buildings need an unobstructed connected pathway to function. Tennis implementation currently applies this general rule; the specific facility edge case still needs original runtime verification.
- Putting Green, Pro Shop and Driving Range (p.19): improve an existing Imagination, Accuracy or Length skill. No absent skill is granted. The text establishes property-level benefit but does not establish the prototype's timed visits, duration or round-limited strength. Those interactions remain provisional pending runtime observation.
- Resort Hotel (p.20): generates well-rested golfers able to play longer courses and stay happier longer; exact stamina effect is not given.

No original executable was run for these observations.

- Ballwashers (p.18): golfers stop to clean their ball, improving accuracy for the remainder of the hole; stops can slow play. The current visitor implementation uses a temporary hole-bound benefit. Exact price, timing, strength and visit-selection rules remain unverified.

Path stamina follow-up: the [archived original-game tips](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/OpenSMSG/OpenSMSG/wiki/Original-SimGolf-Tips) state that paths reduce golfer fatigue. The implementation now reduces energy drain while walking over effective path surfaces, including constructed and starting bridge decks. Waiting/standing receives no path discount. The 0.5 multiplier is provisional; the source supplies no numeric strength. Existing hotel rest benefits still apply. Original fatigue rates, steep-slope complaints and golf carts remain unverified/unimplemented as appropriate.

Steep-path feedback: archived tips also describe complaints about uphill paths steeper than one elevation level. Current visitors now complain once per hole while actually walking uphill between path surfaces. The gradient cutoff is provisionally 0.25 (one editor half-unit elevation step over a two-unit tile); using a gradient avoids overlooking a slope halfway through the segment. Original definition of a level, downhill behavior, repeated-segment reactions and cart handling remain unverified. Complaints use the existing happiness/fee/fun and dandelion-incident system.

Ranger foundation: archived tips describe deploying Rangers/Marshalls at slow tees and an exclamation mark identifying motivated golfers. Rangers are now hireable and deployable through the shared staff controls. An idle Ranger motivates nearby queueing/addressing visitors; this temporarily increases walking and shot-preparation pace. Multiple Rangers do not stack the effect; existing reservations and partner-shot gates still apply. Prototype values are $150 hiring, $12/min wages, 8 world-unit radius, 20 simulation seconds and 1.25 pace factor. Those values, stationary coverage behavior and renewal timing are provisional. Original Marshal upgrade/gates, staff development, behavior toward unhappy golfers, and exact coverage/pacing need verification. No original executable was run.

Staff capacity correction: [SimuLord's 2002 strategy guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/480860-sid-meiers-simgolf/faqs/16147), section 5, reports a sixteen-employee limit and describes staffing plans exceeding the prototype's three employees. A [2004 forum tip](https://www.neoseeker.com/forums/3457/t383797-hints-tips-without-using-cheat-codes/) instead reports twenty. Adopted sixteen as the contemporary guide's working limit; original executable/version verification remains required. This corrects the unsupported three-employee restriction without claiming the conflicting sources establish universal parity. The guide also corroborates advanced employees becoming available after six holes; exact Marshal advantages remain unverified.

Cart Garage: re-read printed p.19 of the supplied manual. It states that the garage reduces waiting and speeds course completion. The contemporary strategy guide's cart section describes paths/fairways as driveable surfaces. Current implementation grants new visitors cart availability when a garage is connected; riding is limited to paths (including bridge decks), fairway and firm fairway, with walking elsewhere. Availability lasts for the booked round; no new rental fee is invented. $2,000 price, three-tile footprint, twofold pace and arrival-time pickup are provisional. Exact unlock/eligibility, pickup/parking, shared carts, professional usage and original animations remain open. The manual does not supply those detailed rules here.


Cart parking implementation update: a cart now has a saved world position independent of its golfer. It remains on driveable ground while the golfer walks away and takes a shot; later routing returns to the cart if both route segments are reachable. This is a functional implementation choice, not a verified reproduction of original pickup or parking rules. There is still one available cart per visitor, no shared occupancy or resident-pro cart usage, and initial availability still comes from the connected garage at round arrival. Terrain edits can strand a cart; the golfer then falls back to an available walking route.


Club Pro source check: visually re-read printed p.20 of the supplied manual. It lists Club Pro among basic employees and describes greeting golfers as improving fun. Printed p.21 lists Celebrity as the stronger skilled greeting role. Implemented Club Pro as a basic hire, with provisional $150 hire cost, $12/min wage, six-world-unit greeting radius and one positive happiness/fun incident per round. Multiple Club Pros do not stack that incident. Stationary area coverage, exact costs, frequency and strength need original-runtime verification; Celebrity remains unfinished.


Celebrity implementation follows printed pp.20–21: skilled employees become available at six or more holes and Celebrity greetings improve fun more than Club Pro greetings. Current direct hire costs $300 with $24/min wages, uses the same six-unit radius, and grants a shared base welcome plus one additional positive reaction per round. A prior Club Pro welcome earns only the additional bonus; later basic greeters add nothing. These numerical and encounter rules are provisional, as is the current direct-hire flow instead of upgrading an existing employee.


Refreshment Consultant implementation follows printed p.21 of the supplied manual: this skilled employee improves attitudes with drinks even if golfers were not originally thirsty. Current implementation shares the vendor service duration and targeting/movement, while also admitting non-thirsty golfers until their consultant benefit has been received for the hole. $300 hire cost, $24/min wage, one positive incident per hole, and a distinct incident from ordinary vendor service are provisional. Original service timing, upgrade flow and numerical effects still require runtime verification.


Existing staff upgrades: printed p.20 describes upgrading to skilled employees at six or more holes. Implemented three supported role mappings through the selected-employee control. Existing work, identity and name are preserved. Charging the difference between basic/skilled hire costs is provisional; original costs, experience requirements and exact job-transition behavior still need runtime verification. Ranger→Marshall is pending the missing angry-golfer/ejection behavior.

Swim Club continuation: implemented the Tropical counterpart identified on printed p.19 as a recreation facility sharing the Tennis Court starting-attitude floor. A connected Swim Club can provide that benefit; owning multiple recreation facilities cannot stack it. The pool, terrace, changing pavilion and parasols are newly modeled in the approved dimensional style. The $2,200 cost, seven-tile footprint and current path requirement remain prototype choices shared with Tennis Court. Both variants are exposed in the current construction palette until regional property availability is integrated; this is not a claim of original regional menu parity. No swimming-service visits, additional fees or golfer skills were invented.

Regional recreation catalog continuation: modeled and integrated Stable and Spa, the Links and Desert variants listed on printed p.19. They share the same provisional $2,200 price, seven-tile footprint and connected starting-attitude floor as Tennis Court/Swim Club. Four named variants are now implemented; their simultaneous availability on Willow Brook is still a prototype deviation until region-driven construction is integrated. The Stable horses are static scenery and do not imply a simulated riding service.

Transport source correction, printed p.20 (PDF physical page 12, visually inspected): Marina increases profits from building lots and the chance an International Celebrity moves into one. Desert replaces Marina with Helipad; Links replaces it with Church. Helipad is therefore an original regional counterpart, not merely a user extension. Airstrip increases green fees per hole, cumulatively with SGA Top 100/Top 18 bonuses; Links replaces it with Castle, Desert with Casino, Tropical with Theme Park. These descriptions do not establish a transport-arrival mechanic. The earlier transport-arrival backlog framing is superseded by these documented economic functions.

Implemented Airstrip fee bonus at completion when connected, with a provisional 25% amount because the manual supplies no numerical multiplier. Each completed score stores its bonus so later demolition/disconnection cannot rewrite historic payments. Pros pay zero; multiple Airstrips do not stack. Exact amount, connection requirement and SGA stacking still require original-runtime confirmation. Marina/Helipad economic effects await building lots and celebrity residency.
